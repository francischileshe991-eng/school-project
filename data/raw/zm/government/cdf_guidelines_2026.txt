# Constituency Development Fund (CDF) Guidelines & Procedures (Zambia)
# Ministry of Local Government and Rural Development (MLGRD)
# Official Public Information Guide

## 1. Overview and Legal Framework
The Constituency Development Fund (CDF) is established under the Constituency Development Fund Act of the Laws of Zambia and administered by the Ministry of Local Government and Rural Development (MLGRD). The annual allocation per constituency has grown steadily across recent national budgets: K1.6 million (2021), K25.7 million (2022), K28.3 million (2023), K30.6 million (2024), K36.1 million (2025), and K40 million (2026). The 2026 allocation of K40 million per constituency brings the national total to approximately K6.245 billion. CDF is designed to decentralize public resources directly to local communities for poverty alleviation, infrastructure modernization, youth and women empowerment, and educational bursaries.

*Note (2026): While the 2027 national budget introduced measures to realign CDF disbursements, the Government clarified that the K40 million per-constituency allocation remains unchanged. VERIFY the latest allocation figures against the annual national budget and the Ministry of Local Government and Rural Development before quoting.*

## 2. The Core Components of CDF

*Note: The percentage splits shown below (50% community projects, 20% empowerment, 20% bursaries, 10% contingency) reflect the commonly applied structure; VERIFY the current official percentages against the latest MLGRD CDF guidelines before relying on them.*

### Component 1: Community Projects (50% Allocation)
Dedicated to infrastructure and civic development within the constituency:
- Construction and expansion of rural health posts, clinics, and maternity annexes.
- Construction and rehabilitation of 1x3 classroom blocks, science laboratories, and teachers' houses.
- Drilling of clean community boreholes, solar water pump schemes, and piped water kiosks.
- Maintenance of rural feeder roads, culverts, crossing bridges, and drainage.
- Construction of local police posts, community halls, civic markets, and street lighting.

### Component 2: Youth, Women, and Community Empowerment (20% Allocation)
Designed to stimulate local economic growth and create sustainable employment:
1. **Empowerment Grants (Non-Repayable):**
   - Direct cash grants awarded to registered youth clubs, women's cooperatives, and vulnerable community groups.
   - Typically up to K40,000 to K50,000 per cooperative or group.
   - Intended for viable income-generating ventures such as poultry, fish farming, piggery, tailoring, metal fabrication, carpentry, horticulture, and village banking capital.
   - Grants do not require repayment to the local council.
2. **Empowerment Soft Loans (Low-Interest Revolving Funds):**
   - Concessionary low-interest business loans (interest rates typically between 5% and 9% per annum) managed through designated commercial financial institutions (e.g., NATSAVE, CEEC, ZANACO).
   - Designed for formal SMEs, cooperatives, and entrepreneurs requiring capital for machinery, equipment, transport, or commercial expansion.
   - Loans are repayable over 12 to 36 months to replenish the revolving fund for other applicants.

### Component 3: Secondary Boarding School & Skills Development Bursaries (20% Allocation)
A vital social protection pillar ensuring equitable educational access:
1. **Secondary School Boarding Bursary:**
   - 100% full boarding bursary covering boarding fees and associated statutory school costs for vulnerable and orphaned pupils enrolled in government public boarding secondary schools across Zambia.
   - The pupil must have an official admission or acceptance letter from a public boarding school.
2. **Skills Development Bursary:**
   - 100% full bursary for youth (aged 16 to 35) admitted to TEVETA-accredited training institutions, trade colleges, and youth resource centers.
   - Covers 100% of tuition fees, boarding and accommodation, examination fees, and starter protective clothing/tools.
   - Courses supported: Automotive mechanics, electrical installation, carpentry and joinery, bricklaying, welding and metal fabrication, cosmetology, plumbing, heavy machinery operations, catering and food production, agriculture, and information technology.
   - Academic prerequisites: For Trade Test Levels, formal Grade 12 credits are NOT mandatory; basic literacy or functional trade interest qualifies the applicant!

### Component 4: Disaster Contingency & Monitoring (10% Allocation)
- Emergency bridge or roof repairs following severe rains, disease outbreak mitigation, and logistical monitoring by the council engineering team.

## 3. Step-by-Step Application Process for Citizens

- **Step 1 (Obtain Free Application Forms):** Official CDF application forms are strictly free and cannot be sold. Collect them from:
  - Your local Ward Development Committee (WDC) Chairperson or Executive.
  - The office of your local Ward Councillor.
  - The Civic Centre / Town Council / Municipal Council offices.
  - The official website or portal of your local district council.
- **Step 2 (Prepare Supporting Documentation):**
  - For Bursaries: Copy of Green National Registration Card (NRC) of applicant and parents/guardians, recommendation letter from local church/civic leader proving vulnerability, and official admission letter from a public boarding school or TEVETA-accredited college.
  - For Grants / Loans: PACRA or Registrar of Cooperatives registration certificate, cooperative constitution, full member list with NRC copies, active business bank account details, and a viable project proposal with a budget.
- **Step 3 (Submission to Ward Development Committee):** Submit all completed documents directly to your Ward Development Committee (WDC).
- **Step 4 (Appraisal & Prioritization):**
  - The WDC reviews and appraises applications from all zones in the ward to ensure fairness and genuine community priority.
  - Shortlisted applications are submitted to the Constituency Development Fund Committee (CDFC).
- **Step 5 (CDFC & Provincial TAC Approval):**
  - The CDFC evaluates the ward submissions, prioritizes funding within the budget, and forwards the recommendations to the Full Council.
  - The Provincial Technical Advisory Committee (TAC) and the Ministry of Local Government and Rural Development provide final statutory clearance.
  - Funds are disbursed directly to institutional accounts (schools/colleges for bursaries, cooperative bank accounts for grants, or partner banks for loans).
